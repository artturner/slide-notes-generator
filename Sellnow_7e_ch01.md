--- SLIDE 5 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Defining Communication

**Body:**
* **Communication:** A complex process of expressing, interpreting, and coordinating messages with others.
* **Goals of Communication:**
    * Create shared meaning.
    * Meet social goals.
    * Manage personal identity.
    * Carry out relationships.
* **Messages:** Verbal utterances, visual images, and nonverbal behaviors used to convey thoughts and feelings.

**Speaker Notes:**
Communication isn't just "talking." It is a strategic process we use to navigate our world. Whether you are using words, emojis, or body language, you are creating a "message" to achieve a specific goal.

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Bulleted list defining communication and its goals.

--- SLIDE 6 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Encoding and Decoding

**Body:**
* **Encoding:** The process of creating messages.
* **Decoding:** The process of interpreting messages.
* **Feedback:** A response message indicating how the initial message was interpreted.
* **Role of "Scripts":**
    * **Canned Plan:** A "mental library" of scripts based on what has worked in the past.
    * **Script:** The actual text of what to say and do in a specific situation.

**Speaker Notes:**
We don't usually start from scratch every time we speak. We access a "canned plan" in our brain—like a mental file cabinet—and pull out a "script." For example, you have a specific script for greeting your grandmother that is different from the script you use to greet your best friend.

--- SLIDE 8 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** The Communication Context

**Body:**
Communication is influenced by the **Context** (the situation surrounding the event):
1.  **Physical:** Location, environment (noise, light), and proximity.
2.  **Social:** The nature of the relationship between participants.
3.  **Historical:** Previous communication episodes between participants.
4.  **Psychological:** Moods and feelings brought to the encounter.
5.  **Cultural:** Beliefs, values, and rituals of a specific culture.

**Speaker Notes:**
You cannot understand a message in isolation. "I love you" means something very different depending on the *Social* context (mother vs. date) and the *Physical* context (private dinner vs. public joke).

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** A list of the five dimensions of communication context: Physical, Social, Historical, Psychological, and Cultural.

--- SLIDE 9 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Communication Settings

**Body:**
* **Intrapersonal:** Interactions in our own minds (self-talk).
* **Interpersonal:** Informal interaction between two people with an identifiable relationship.
* **Group:** 3 to 20 people interacting (families, work teams).
* **Public:** One participant delivers a message to a group of more than 20.
* **Mass:** Communication delivered through mass media to large segments of the population.

**Speaker Notes:**
We move fluently between these settings. You might be engaging in *intrapersonal* communication (thinking about lunch) while listening to *public* communication (this lecture).

--- SLIDE 11 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Three Sub-Processes of Communication

**Body:**
The process consists of three interrelated activities:
1.  **Message Production (Encoding):** Forming goals, recalling scripts, and adapting them.
2.  **Message Interpretation (Decoding):** Reading/listening, observing nonverbals, and interpreting based on context.
3.  **Interaction Coordination:** Behavioral adjustments made by participants to create shared meaning.

**Speaker Notes:**
Interaction coordination is crucial—it's how we adjust on the fly. If you tell a joke and no one laughs, you adjust your behavior immediately (perhaps by explaining it or changing the subject).

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** List of the three sub-processes: Message Production, Message Interpretation, and Interaction Coordination.

--- SLIDE 12 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Channels

**Body:**
* **Channel:** The route traveled by the message and the means of transportation.
* **Types:**
    * **Verbal Symbols:** Words.
    * **Nonverbal Cues:** Gestures, tone, movement.
    * **Visual Images:** Emojis, GIFs, pictures.
* **Technology:** Often relies heavily on visuals (emojis, acronyms) to replace nonverbal cues lost in text.

**Speaker Notes:**
In face-to-face communication, the "channel" includes sound waves (voice) and light waves (sight). Online, the channel might be WiFi or cellular data, but we use tools like emojis to replicate the emotional context of physical gestures.

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Definition of Channel and list of types: Verbal, Nonverbal, and Visual.

--- SLIDE 13 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Media Richness and Synchronicity

**Body:**
* **Media Richness:** How much information can be transmitted?
    * **Rich:** Live, in-person (all cues available).
    * **Lean:** Text only (few cues, higher chance of misunderstanding).
* **Synchronicity:** The timing of feedback.
    * **Synchronous:** Real-time (Face-to-face, Zoom, Phone).
    * **Asynchronous:** Lag time (Email, Text, Social Media posts).

**Speaker Notes:**
"Rich" channels are usually better for complex or difficult conversations. "Lean" channels are efficient for simple logistics. "Asynchronous" channels give you time to edit your message before sending.

**Title:** Interference (Noise)

**Body:**
**Interference:** Any stimulus that interferes with shared meaning.

| Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Noise** | External sights or sounds that distract from the message. | A cell phone ringing, construction noise, a pop-up ad. |
| **Psychological Noise** | Thoughts and feelings that compete for attention. | Daydreaming, hunger, feeling offended by a speaker's language. |

**Speaker Notes:**
Noise isn't just sound. If you are hungry during a meeting, your internal hunger pangs are "psychological noise" preventing you from listening effectively.

--- SLIDE 20 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Communication is Continuous and Irreversible

**Body:**
* **Continuous:** We are always encoding/decoding. Even silence sends a message.
    * *Example:* Shivering signals cold; a yawn signals boredom.
* **Irreversible:** You cannot "un-communicate" a message.
    * **Digital Footprints:** Online posts leave a permanent record.
    * **Privacy:** Emails and texts can be forwarded without your consent.

**Speaker Notes:**
"You cannot not communicate." Even if you sit silently in the back of the class with your arms crossed, you are communicating disinterest or defiance. Regarding irreversibility: Apologies can repair damage, but they cannot erase the original message from the listener's memory or the internet.

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Bullets explaining that communication is continuous (always happening) and irreversible (cannot be erased).

--- SLIDE 21 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Communication is Situated and Indexical

**Body:**
* **Situated:** Meaning depends on the specific setting.
    * *Example:* Swearing might be okay at a poker game (situated) but not at a grandmother's birthday dinner.
* **Indexical:** Messages measure the "emotional temperature" of a relationship.
    * **Trust:** Belief you can rely on the partner.
    * **Control:** Who is "in charge"? (Signaled by *Complementary* or *Symmetrical* feedback).
    * **Intimacy:** Degree of emotional closeness and disclosure.

**Speaker Notes:**
"Indexical" means the communication serves as an "Index" or meter reading of the relationship. If you constantly interrupt someone, you are indexing a struggle for *Control*. If you share a secret, you are indexing *Intimacy*.

- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Definitions of Situated (context-dependent) and Indexical (measure of relationship trust, control, and intimacy).

**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Ethical Principles

**Body:**
Ethical communicators are:
1.  **Honest:** Truthful, though "strategic ambiguity" may be used to save face.
2.  **Act with Integrity:** Consistency between beliefs and actions (no hypocrisy).
3.  **Fair:** Impartial; gathering all facts before judging.
4.  **Respectful:** Showing positive regard for others' rights and feelings.
5.  **Responsible:** Recognizing the power of words to hurt or heal.

**Speaker Notes:**
Integrity is "walking the walk." If you claim to value teamwork but refuse to do your share of the project, you lack communication integrity.

--- SLIDE 25 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Bright Side vs. Dark Side Messages

**Body:**
* **Bright Side Messages:** Ethical and appropriate.
    * *Examples:* Compliments, gratitude, supportive statements.
* **Dark Side Messages:** Unethical and/or inappropriate.
    * **Accusations/Blame:** "It's all your fault."
    * **Critical Judgments:** "You are terrible at this."
    * **Deception:** Lies or withholding info.
    * **Bullying:** Name-calling, spreading rumors (in-person or cyber).

**Speaker Notes:**
Dark side messages damage relationships and self-esteem. Note that a message can be "honest" but still be "dark side" if it is brutally critical without purpose.

--- SLIDE 29 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Defining Competence

**Body:**
**Communication Competence:** The impression that behavior is both **Appropriate** and **Effective**.
* **Appropriate:** Conforms to expectations of the situation.
* **Effective:** Achieves the goals of the interaction.

**Requirements for Competence:**
1.  **Motivation:** Wanting to improve.
2.  **Knowledge:** Knowing what to do.
3.  **Skills:** Practicing the behavior.
4.  **Credibility:** Perceived knowledge, trustworthiness, and warmth.
5.  **Social Ease:** Managing anxiety.

**Speaker Notes:**
You can be effective but inappropriate (e.g., getting a refund by screaming at a cashier). You can be appropriate but ineffective (e.g., being so polite you never ask for what you need). Competence requires BOTH.

**Accessibility:**
- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Definition of Competence and list of requirements: Motivation, Knowledge, Skills, Credibility, and Social Ease.

--- SLIDE 30 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Communication Apprehension (CA)

**Body:**
**Glossophobia:** Fear of public speaking (75% of adults).

**Four Types of Apprehension:**
1.  **Traitlike CA:** Anxious in most speaking situations (~20% of people).
2.  **Audience-based CA:** Anxious only with specific people (e.g., a boss).
3.  **Situational CA:** Short-lived anxiety during a specific encounter (e.g., a job interview).
4.  **Context-based CA:** Anxiety in a particular setting (e.g., public speaking).

**Speaker Notes:**
Most people have some form of CA. It is normal. The goal isn't to eliminate it entirely, but to manage it so you can display "Social Ease."

- **Alt Text for [Slide Content]:** Definitions of Glossophobia and the four types of Communication Apprehension.

--- SLIDE 31 ---
**Type:** Content Slide
**Title:** Communication Improvement Plans

**Body:**
To become competent, you need a plan.
1.  **Identify the Problem:** Be specific.
    * *Example:* "I bury my head in my notes during presentations."
2.  **State the Specific Goal:**
    * *Example:* "I will look at the audience more directly."
3.  **Outline Procedure:** Specific steps to practice.
    * *Example:* "I will practice aloud standing up; I will pretend objects are people."
4.  **Measure Progress:** How will you know you succeeded?
    * *Example:* "I maintained eye contact 50% of the time."

**Speaker Notes:**
This is the "So What?" of the chapter. We don't just learn theory; we apply it to get better.
